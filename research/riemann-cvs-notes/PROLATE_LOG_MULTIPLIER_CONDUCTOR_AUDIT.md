# Audit correction: the prolate tail is not the fixed-Hermite boundary layer

## Why this note is necessary

`LOG_MULTIPLIER_BOUNDARY_LAYER_REDUCTION.md` records an exact and useful scaling
identity for a spatially localized profile of width
\(\delta_\lambda=\lambda^{-2}\) in logarithmic coordinates.  That model is
appropriate for the Gaussian fixed-Hermite exterior tail.

It must **not** be assumed, without a separate theorem, that the optimal
fixed-index prolate leakage has the same spatial profile.  A time-limited
prolate vector has an exterior Fourier leakage generated by endpoint data; its
far tail is oscillatory and typically algebraic in the exterior coordinate.
The rapid scale is a carrier frequency of order the prolate bandwidth, rather
than necessarily a spatial layer of width \(\lambda^{-2}\).

The preferred prolate-to-Weil reduction is therefore the exact conductor
identity below.

---

## 1. Exact tail algebra

Let \(P\) be the time cutoff, \(Q=1-P\), and let \(\mathcal F\) be the Fourier
involution on the real-even ambient Hilbert space.  Let

\[
P\mathcal Fp
=
\varepsilon\sigma p,
\qquad
\|p\|=1,
\qquad
\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\},
\qquad
0<\sigma<1,
\]

and define

\[
t=Q\mathcal Fp,
\qquad
d=1-\sigma^2=\|t\|^2.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal Fp
=
\varepsilon\sigma p+t.
\]

Applying \(\mathcal F\) once more gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Ft
=
dp-\varepsilon\sigma t.
}
\]

This is formalized in `ProlateConductorIdentity.lean`.

---

## 2. Exact logarithmic conductor identity

Let

\[
M=\log|x|
\]

be the physical multiplication form, and let

\[
\mathcal H=M+\mathcal FM\mathcal F
\]

be the logarithmic conductor model for the archimedean term.  Since \(p\) and
\(t\) have disjoint retained/exterior supports, multiplication by \(M\) has no
cross term between them.  Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle t,\mathcal FM\mathcal Ft\rangle
&=
\langle\mathcal Ft,M\mathcal Ft\rangle \\
&=
d^2\langle p,Mp\rangle
+
\sigma^2\langle t,Mt\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\langle t,\mathcal Ht\rangle
=
(1+\sigma^2)\langle t,Mt\rangle
+d^2\langle p,Mp\rangle.
}
\]

Equivalently, because \(\sigma^2=1-d\),

\[
\langle t,\mathcal Ht\rangle
=
(2-d)\langle t,Mt\rangle
+d^2\langle p,Mp\rangle.
\]

This formula is exact and uses no assumed boundary profile.

---

## 3. What remains to prove

On the exterior support \(|x|\ge\lambda\),

\[
\langle t,Mt\rangle
\ge
\log\lambda\,d.
\]

To obtain a two-sided common-leading estimate, it is enough to prove a coarse
logarithmic moment bound

\[
\boxed{
\langle t,\log|x|\,t\rangle
\le
K\log\lambda\,d
}
\]

for a fixed constant \(K\) on the finite set of fixed-index modes used in the
parity argument.  One does **not** need to identify an exact limiting profile
or prove the coefficient is exactly two.

The retained correction has an additional factor \(d^2\).  It is enough to
show

\[
|\langle p,Mp\rangle|
\le
C\log\lambda
\]

or even a fixed polynomial bound, because \(d\) is exponentially small.

Under these estimates,

\[
\log\lambda\,d
\lesssim
\langle t,\mathcal Ht\rangle
\lesssim
(2K+o(1))\log\lambda\,d.
\]

A fixed condition number already preserves the
\(\lambda^{-4}\) exact-parity prolate advantage.

## 4. Plausible analytic tools for the tail moment

The required upper logarithmic moment should be attacked through the actual
fixed-index prolate exterior asymptotics, not by importing the fixed-Hermite
spatial layer.

Useful equivalent targets include:

1. a uniform bound on the normalized exterior logarithmic moment,
   \[
   \frac1d
   \int_{|x|\ge\lambda}
   \log\!\left(\frac{|x|}{\lambda}\right)|t(x)|^2dx
   =O(\log\lambda);
   \]

2. a uniform exterior asymptotic of endpoint-oscillatory type with an
   integrable \(1/|x|\)-type envelope;

3. a Mellin-transform estimate for the exterior PSWF continuation;

4. a differential-equation comparison for the prolate continuation beyond the
   turning/singular endpoint.

The fixed-index Fuchs asymptotic controls the total exterior mass but does not,
by itself, control this logarithmic moment.  A source must be checked for the
needed uniform exterior estimate.
